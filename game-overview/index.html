Game overview

Plot summary

The world is a promising place, though not without its perils.

In an alternate-history Earth, governments cooperate to serve their citizens as well as possible. Wars are a thing of the past, and nobody is left without nourishment, shelter, or education. Science is universally valued, policies preserve the health of the climate, and technology facilitates both exploration of the solar system and personal health and enhancement. While governments still want to look their best and take credit for humanity’s successes, conflict never escalates beyond argument and the occasional espionage scandal.

The Sun, however, has begun to grow more unstable. Its sunspot cycles, which have always lasted around 11 years, are now terminating with ever more violent solar storms. Each time these flare-ups happen, more and more of civilization’s technology is suppressed or destroyed. It has reached the point where all governments recognize the Sun as the most important issue of their times, and scientific progress towards addressing the effects of the solar flares has been elevated to the highest priority. The survival of a technological society is at stake.

The solar system is a promising place, though not without its perils. Will you lead humanity successfully through them?

Game summary

Cyclicity is a campaign-style board game with strong narrative and role-play elements. It is designed to be played by 6 players over the course of approximately 12–15 games (each game representing a single sunspot cycle, from its quiet beginning to its destructive end). The players take on the roles of government ambassadors to the world’s highest scientific assembly, which debates and prioritizes technological and theoretical initiatives and advancements—all with the ultimate goal of helping humanity overcome the Sun’s increasing aggression.

The main game mechanic is based on Horrible Guild’s Dilemma Card System™, where players vote on decisions drawn from an ever-changing deck. These votes are supplemented by a modest and evolving set of worker-placement actions—some of them requiring cooperation to implement, others benefiting only the player taking the action. In true legacy-game style, many game components will be written on, marked, and destroyed as the campaign progresses.

Each role comes with a set of mechanical goals and preferences, with additional ones acquired as the campaign progresses. Each player uses these (sometimes conflicting or indefinite) goals as a foundation for the role they choose to play during the discussions and votes. The roles are further enriched by a fully formed game setting whose history is continuously being revealed during play. Cyclicity shines brightest when players combine strategy with narrative—when they not only try to shape the game’s outcomes in favor of their mechanical objectives, but also embody their role’s perspective and create a vivid and lasting persona for other players to enjoy (and sometimes resent!).
