Homo species

Science now recognizes that evolution separately produced several species of hominids within the Homo genus at various times and places in history. The most well-known of these species is Homo neanderthalensis, with their stereotypical low craniums and foreheads, protruding jaws, large rib cages and wide hands; they also matured much more quickly than Homo sapiens. In many ways, Homo denisova were halfway between Neanderthals and modern humans in terms of their foreheads, large teeth, and maturation rate, although Denisovan skulls were wider than either species’ and they tended to be ambush predators instead of relying on pure aggression. Homo naledi were equally powerful but taller instead of broader, with androgynous features and strong tribal instincts. Last and (in size) least, the Homo floresiensis were much shorter than other hominids, but otherwise proportioned like Homo sapiens, and were nimble and clever hunters instead of overpowering ones. The general consensus is that Homo sapiens has been the only extant hominid species for the past ten or twenty thousand years.
