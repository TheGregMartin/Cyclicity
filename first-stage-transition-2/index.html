Second Stage Transition

This event is the transition from the Solar Stage into the Interstellar Stage. The event will take place over three rounds, where each player maneuvers for joint control of the three outposts, secures their existing installations … and possibly steals other players’ installations during the scramble.

Setup

Before beginning the event, mark one box on every installation with the stage transition icon. Immediately destroy all such installations that have both of their boxes marked.

Remove all installations from your mat, keeping track of which ones are in your Inner, Middle, and Outer Zones. Flip the installation mat upside down, oriented so that the numbers are readable to you (with 1 closest to you). Put your installations on this side of the mat, with Inner Zone installations on the 1 arc, Middle Zone installations on the 2 arc, and Outer Zone installations on the 3 arc. (Their horizontal placement doesn’t matter; it might help to have the installations spread as far apart as possible.) If you have too many Inner Zone installations to fit them all on the 1 arc, move one or two of your choice to the 2 arc.

All players position their installation mats around the cards and supply ship reserve on the table, so that the six mats form a hexagon around them. Place any installation explanation cards adjacent to your installation mat, available for everyone to see, as well as your ranking card from the previous game. Keep your drone reserve behind your polity screen.

Description of play

At the beginning of each of the three rounds, each player starts with a certain amount of supply ships that they can allocate during that round. That amount (which is the same for each of the rounds) is 8, plus half the number of installations they start with (rounded down), plus half their polity’s EGO. [LINK] On a player’s turn, they may spend any or all of their supply ships for that round to place drones on various stations and/or installations. The number of supply ships needed to place a drone on various locations is listed on the Cost Reference card in the middle of the table.

Once an installation has at least one drone on it, it is provisionally controlled by the player who has placed the most drones there so far. Put that player’s drones on top of the installation and other players’ drones beside that installation, to help track who controls it at the moment. Control only changes to a new player if that player has more drones on the installation—tying for the most drones is not enough, and the player already controlling it retains control. (An installation with no drones on it is controlled by its owner.)

Each outpost is jointly controlled by the two players who have placed the most drones on it. If the two players controlling an outpost have an equal number of drones there, it’s important to keep track of which player reached that drone total first, in case a third player exceeds that number of drones and one of the first two players must be ousted. It’s recommended to have the first player’s drones on the title of the outpost and the second player’s drones on the text description.

Take a few minutes to look at all the players’ installations, and take note any installations you might be interested in stealing! (It might help to record their actual installation numbers privately.)

The order of play is as follows:

  • In the first round, the player with the 1st-place ranking card goes first, followed by the 2nd-place player, and so on, ending with the 6th-place player.
  • The second round goes in the reverse order: the player with the 6th-place ranking card goes first, followed by the 5th-place player, and so on, ending with the 1st-place player.
  • After the second round has been completed, turn over the card on top of the remainder of Envelope C1, and follow its instructions to carry out the third and final round of this event.

Note: Supply ships cannot change hands during this event—they are only there to determine how many actions a player can take each round. There is no bribing during stage transitions, and any agreements among players are non-binding.

Note: Players are not limited by the number of physical drones in their reserve. They can, for instance, use supply ships themselves in place of drones on their own arc 1 installations.
