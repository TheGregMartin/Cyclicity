Tutorial branch point #1

This portion of the tutorial uses the branch point card T.2.

  1. Greg: In the actual game these branch point cards would be shuffled, but for the tutorial we’ll do them in order. The front of each branch point card presents a scenario where a decision is needed, and two possible choices (0 and 1) that you will decide between by voting. The back of the card, which we should never look at until the vote is decided, describes the outcome of the decision.
  2. Vivien: [reads the first branch point card aloud]
  3. Greg: Note how these symbols indicate a slider that will go up if that decision is made—artificial intelligence for the 0 choice, and genetics for the 1 choice. I’ll put these 0 and 1 tokens near those sliders as a reminder during the discussion and voting.
  4. Janelle: I’ll vote for option 1, because [make something up]. I’ll deploy my Voting Tile with the 1 side up, and put two of my available drones on it.
  5. Greg: When you deploy your Voting Tile, you put as many drones as you want on it. Remember to take those drones from behind your screen, not your drone reserve (which is just a pile of tokens that aren’t in play but might enter play later).
  6. Greg: Also, the Sponsor tile is always held by the player with the most drones on their voting tile. So I’ll give Janelle the Sponsor tile now. It’s best to keep the Sponsor tile on the right-hand side of your other stuff.
  7. Katie: I choose not to vote because [make something up]. I’ll deploy my Abstain tile.
  8. Greg: On your first turn of every vote, you have to either deploy a Voting Tile with one or more drones, or else deploy your Abstain tile and take some non-voting action. Right now the only available actions are the Drone Factory and the Mining Operations on your installation mat. (You’re allowed to take no action, but those two actions are almost always better than passing.)
  9. Katie: I’ll put one drone from behind my screen on my Drone Factory.
  10. Barney: I want to vote for option 1 as well, because [make something up]. I’ll deploy my Voting Tile with the 1 side up, but just put one of my available drones on it.
  11. Jason: I’m going to vote for option 0, because [make something up]. I’ll deploy my Voting Tile with the 0 side up, and put three of my drones on it.
  12. Greg: Since Jason now has the most drones on their voting tile, the Sponsor tile passes to them.
  13. Graham: I’m going to abstain because [make something up]. I’ll deploy my Abstain tile, and put one drone on my Mining Operations.
  14. Vivien: I’m also voting for option 0, because [make something up]. I’ll deploy my Voting Tile with the 0 side up, but just put one drone on it.
  15. Greg: If the vote were to end right now, the 0 side would be the winner, four drones to three. However, votes don’t end until the person currently holding the Sponsor tile would be taking their turn. Since Jason has the Sponsor tile, you’ll all keep taking turns for now.
  16. Janelle: I feel like I’ve already done my voting duty, so I’m going to put one of my available drones on my Drone Factory.
  17. Greg: When it’s your turn and you’ve already deployed a Voting Tile, you can either add more drones to your vote (without switching sides), or you can take a non-voting action like Janelle did.
  18. Katie: I’m not involved in this vote, so I’m going to put a second drone on my Drone Factory.
  19. Greg: When it’s your turn and you’ve already deployed your Abstain Tile, you can only take non-voting actions for the rest of that vote.
  20. Barney: Well I want my side to win this vote, so I choose to add two more drones to my voting tile.
  21. Greg: Note that Barney has the same number of drones on their voting tile as Jason; but the Sponsor tile only moves if someone has more drones on their voting tile than the current sponsor—ties don’t count. So the Sponsor Tile stays where it is.
  22. Jason: And next it would be my turn, but I’m the one holding the Sponsor Tile; so that means that this vote is actually finished.
  23. Greg: That's right! Note how keeping the Sponsor tile on your right-hand side helps indicate when the voting will stop. And so the 1 side won this vote, five drones to four. (How clever of Barney to realize that they could dictate the outcome with their second action!)
  24. Greg: As soon as a vote is complete, we need to clear all the drones used that round and finalize the Sponsor for the decision we just made. First, everybody on the losing side of the vote gets to take all their voting drones back behind their screen—losing a vote doesn’t cost you anything but pride.
  25. Vivien: So I take my 0 voting tile back, and the drone on it goes back behind my screen so I can use it again later.
  26. Jason: And I get all three of my drones back, thankfully.
  27. Greg: If the person holding the Sponsor tile had been on the winning side, they would be the decision’s final Sponsor. But here Jason was on the losing side. In this case, the decision’s final Sponsor is the player on the winning side with the most drones on their voting tile.
  28. Barney: That’s me, I get the Sponsor tile!
  29. Greg: Now let me explain how drone removal works for the winning side. Each winning voter takes one of their voting drones back behind their screen; but the rest of their voting drones go into their drone reserve.
  30. Janelle: So one of these drones stays available to me, while the other one goes out of play.
  31. Barney: And this vote cost me two of my drones, but I can take the third one back behind my screen.
  32. Greg: This is a very common mechanic in Cyclicity—if you use drones for some action, at the end of each branch point vote you’ll get one of these drones back behind your screen, while the rest of them go into your drone reserve. So actions tend to “cost” one less drone than the number of drones you play. If you play a single drone somewhere, you’ll get it back and that action didn’t cost you any drones.
  33. Graham: Losing a vote is an exception to that—Jason and Vivien got all their drones back. And it looks like the Drone Factory and Mining Operations are also exceptions….
  34. Greg: That’s right—drones can stay on those two spots for as long as you want, and you can remove them at any time (even if it’s not your turn, and even right before you use them for some other action).
  35. Katie: I’m leaving my drones on my Drone Factory for now—if I put one more drone there, I’ll get a bonus drone when I remove all three at once.
  36. Graham: Same with me, if I put a second drone on my Mining Operations, I’ll get a free supply ship when I remove them both.
  37. Greg: Okay, we’ve cleaned up after the vote—now let’s learn the consequences of the decision we just made. Note that if you held the branch point card by the red 0 and flipped it vertically, you’d see the 0 outcome at the top of the back of the card, But here we should hold the branch point card by the blue 1 and flip it horizontally, so that we can see the 1 outcome at the top of the card’s back.
  38. Barney: [read the 1 outcome text on the back of the card]
  39. Greg: Check out these icons and numbers in the 1 outcome. The first one says that 18 months will pass between this branch point and the next one. We mark that on this months track (note that each row is half a year). Since solar cycles last between 9 and 14 years, the game will end when the months marker is somewhere in the orange zone—we’ll learn more about that later.
  40. Greg: The second icon says that the genetics slider goes up by 4, which we mark with this slider. In addition, every time sliders go up, they gain momentum while other sliders lose momentum. Specifically, any slider that goes up from a branch point outcome has its momentum set to +2; any slider that doesn’t go up has its momentum reduced by 1—so these other five sliders go from momentum 0 to momentum –1.
  41. Greg: Usually there will be another icon with a treat for the Sponsor, but we have to do more of the tutorial for that.
  42. Barney: Dammit….
