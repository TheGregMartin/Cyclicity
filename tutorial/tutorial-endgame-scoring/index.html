Tutorial endgame scoring

  1. Greg: Let’s go ahead and figure out everyone’s final Victory Points score in this practice game. (Don’t worry, everything will reset for the start of the campaign.) This End-Game Scoring Order gives an outline for calculating people’s scores.
  2. Greg: Before the scoring itself begins, we all carry out end-of-game effects from active rule cards and installations. For example, Katie gains two supply ships at this point from her installation 3C.
  3. Greg: Don’t mark your polity screens now since this is a practice game, but in real games you would start by marking boxes as appropriate for your Polity Dispositions and Polity Achievements (and gain any rewards that you earned by finishing rows of boxes). We do this early in end-game scoring because some VP-related rules might arise that interact with this box-marking.
  4. Greg: Next, we record with any Victory Points, or VP, coming from miscellaneous sources like rule cards or installations. (The word “overfilling” refers to a rule that won’t be relevant for the first few games of the campaign.) For most of the scoring procedure, players should just update their VP totals simultaneously.
  5. Greg: So for example, Vivien gets two VP from her installation, and Barney gets one VP from his. Go ahead and put your standees on the month tracker, which is now a scoring track, to indicate those VP. Everyone else can either put their standee at the #0 spot, or wait until you get VP to introduce your standee.
  6. Greg: Next, everyone counts how many drones they ended the game with. This End-Game Information table on the back of the card shows that the three players with the most remaining drones get VP, which is counted now. When calculating Victory Points, all ties are friendly: for example, if two players tied for having the most drones, they would both get 4 VP, and the player with the next-highest drone count would get the third-place reward of 1VP.
  7. Greg: Similarly, everyone counts how many supply ships they ended the game with. Each player’s ranking gives them a certain number of VP, according to the Supply Contracts card they selected for this game. Go ahead and score those VP now. Again, all ties are friendly: if one player had 9 supply ships, three players had 6 supply ships, and two players had 4 supply ships, there would be one 1st-place finisher, three 2nd-place finishers, and two 5th-place finishers.
  8. Greg: The last sources of Victory Points to be counted are the Classified Polity Objective cards. For maximum drama, players count these last VP one player at a time, with the order determined by everyone’s partial VP scores to this point: the player provisionally ranked in 6th place goes first, then the player provisionally ranked in 5th place, and so on.
  9. Greg: Now all the Victory Points have been tallied, and we learn who the winner is and what the rankings are! The End-Game Information side of the card says how to break ties at this stage—every game will end with the players ranked in 1st place, 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 5th, and 6th place for that game.
  10. Greg: The higher you place, the more Progress Points you’ll get to mark on the left panel of your polity screen. Progress Points are a campaign-spanning measure of achievement. At the start of the campaign you won’t know exactly why they’re good—but trust me, they are definitely good!
  11. Greg: We’ll learn the exact details of those Progress Points at the end of the first game, as well as a couple of ways that these final rankings impact the setup for the following game.
  12. Greg: But for now, you have completed the tutorial and are totally ready to play the first game of your Cyclicity campaign—congratulations! Remember that the rulebook and reference can be found on the same Cyclicity website that this tutorial is on. Does anyone have any questions they want to ask at this point?
