Tutorial envelope 2B

  1. Greg: The first thing we do is give installation 3C to the Sponsor of the just-finished branch point decision, which is Katie in this case. Most of the time, opening an envelope will give an installation to the Sponsor.
  2. Katie:  By the gravity rules, there’s only one place I can put this installation, which is on the leftmost square of my Inner Zone…. Wait, why would it give me supply ships at the end of the game—isn’t that too late to be useful?
  3. Greg: For bribing, yes it’s too late; but supply ships will factor into the end-of-game scoring, which we’ll be talking about as we go through this envelope.
  4. Greg: The main contribution to your end-game scores will come from objective cards. I’m going to give you each two Classified Polity Objective cards now. In a real game you would draft these at the beginning and keep them secret; but for the tutorial, I’m just going to deal you each two cards starting with the Sponsor, and you can put them face up in front of your screens. [Greg deals these players these cards]
    • Katie: Geneticist, Restrictor
    • Barney: Extremist, Spendthrift
    • Jason: Concentrator, Enabler
    • Graham: Patron, Specialist
    • Vivien: Pragmatist, Theoretician
    • Janelle: Balancer, Idler
  5. Greg: Each Classified Polity Objective card has a formula or table for giving you some number of Victory Pointsat the end of the game. (Ignore the upside-down text at the bottom of the cards for now.) These will imply certain goals during the game for you if you want to get as many Victory Points as possible. You might want to make a specific slider high or low, or to not let all sliders get too high or too low; or you might want to have many or few branch point decisions during the game, or to have a branch point decision that takes a large number or small number of months.
  6. Greg: Let’s look at the icons at the top of these cards. All of them have icons that look like briefcasesat the top—ignore those for a moment. An important rule about Classified Polity Objectives is that you’re not allowed to have the same non-briefcase icon on both of your objectives.
  7. Barney: Wuh oh, both of my cards have this equalizer-looking iconon them. I guess that’s because they both deal with all the sliders at once.
  8. Vivien: Me too, both of mine have the theoretical research iconon them. And the objectives are exact opposites—one wants that slider to be low, the other wants it to be high!
  9. Greg: The rule that the two cards can’t have the same icon is partially to prevent conflicts like that; but it’s also to make sure that the two objectives are generally independent of each other and nobody is doubling down on a single goal. Let’s fix the problem by having Barney and Vivien give each other one objective card each.
  10. Barney and Vivien: [trade the Spendthrift and Theoretician cards with each other]
  11. Greg: Now let’s talk about the Priorityicons—each Classified Polity Objective has one, two, or three of them. Partially they indicate roughly how generous each objective is: a Priority-3 objective gives more Victory Points on average than a Priority-2 objective, which gives more than a Priority-1 objective.
  12. Greg: But also, they dictate your third objective of each game, which is a polity Supply Contracts card. [Greg hands these out now]
  13. Greg: Look through your Supply Contracts cards; you’ll see that they’re all generally the same—they give you more Victory Points the more supply ships you end the game with—but they come in different strengths, from three briefcases down to two, down to one and even zero briefcases.
  14. Greg: Once you’ve chosen your Classified Polity Objectives for a game, you’ll then select whichever Supply Contracts card brings your total number of briefcases to six. In this way you can decide how relatively important you want Classified Polity Objectives and Supply Contracts to be. Again, in a real game these would be kept secret, but for the tutorial just put them face up in front of you as well. I suggest putting your unused Supply Contracts cards face down underneath your drone reserve.
  15. Vivien: Ok, so I already have 6 briefcases on my two Classified Polity Objective cards, 3 on each of them; so I select the Priority-0 Supply Contracts. This game I don’t care at all how many supply ships I end up with.
  16. Barney: I have a total of 5 briefcases on my two cards currently, so I select the Priority-1 Supply Contracts.
  17. Janelle: I have 4 briefcases on my two cards, so I select the Priority-2 Supply Contracts; looks like Katie does the same.
  18. Graham: I’m ending up with my biggest Supply Contracts card, since I only have 3 briefcases on my two cards. I’m going to be greedy for bribes this game!
  19. Jason: Wuh oh, my two Classified Polity Objective cards only have 2 briefcases total, 1 on each of them; what happens now?
  20. Greg: In that situation, you just take your highest Supply Contracts card—the one with 3 briefcases. This is a legal combination, but it might be a little disadvantageous.
  21. Jason: Let the record show the tutorial forced me to do this!
  22. Greg: The next card from the envelope is the End-Game Scoring Order card. We’ll go through it at the end of this tutorial; roughly speaking, after miscellaneous Victory Points from things like installations, there are a few Victory Points to be had from having the most drones at the end of the game; then everyone scores Victory Points from their Supply Contracts; and finally everyone scores their two Classified Polity Objectives.
  23. Greg: The next card from this envelope is a tracking card; it tells us when to mark one of its boxes (in this case, after every branch point decision) and then tells us what happens once all the boxes are marked (in this case, a new envelope). Tracking cards like this will pop up during the campaign.
  24. Greg: Lastly, we add these last two cards from the envelope to the branch point deck … and finally, we’re allowed to shuffle that deck—so the next branch point will be random like it should be!
