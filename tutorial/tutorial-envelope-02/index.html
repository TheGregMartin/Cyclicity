Tutorial envelope 02

  1. Greg: Typically, every envelope we open will have a list of instructions as its first card. For example, the first thing we do right now is, everyone gains three drones from their reserve!
  2. Greg: We’re going to learn about installationsnow. Installations are beneficial tokens that you typically get when you Sponsor a branch point decision, or achieve some polity goal and so on, and which persist from game to game. We’ll start by learning where to place installations; once we have several in front of us, we’ll look at them more closely to see what they do.
  3. Graham: Okay, it says the player two to the left of the Sponsor—that’s me!—places installations 6E and 8F in the Inner Zone of my installation mat.
  4. Greg: Yes indeed, place them icons-side up. One standing rule about installations is: they are always placed as far to the left as possible. You can put those in either order, it doesn’t matter.
  5. Vivien: Next it says that I—the player three to the left of the Sponsor—places installations 5B and 9D in two different Zones of my installation mat.
  6. Greg: Another important standing rule about installations is the “gravity rule”: every installation in a higher row has to be above an installation in the row below it (or otherwise it “falls down” because of gravity). Right now, the bottom row of your installation mats is completely inactive, so the Inner Zone is the “ground” when it comes to the gravity rule.
  7. Vivien: So I put my first installation on the leftmost square in my Inner Zone; and since I’ve been told to put them in different Zones, my only choice is to put the second installation on the leftmost square in my Middle Zone.
  8. Greg: That’s right—they have to go as far left as possible, and an installation in your Outer Zone would fall down to the Middle Zone without something underneath it to support it.
  9. Janelle: In a way both of those rules are gravity-like rules: things always fall down, and they also always “fall” to the left.
  10. Greg: Fair point! One difference, though, is that once you choose which Zone to put an installation in, it will stay in that Zone forever (unless something in the game instructs otherwise). But within a Zone, the left-to-right order will never matter, and you can freely swap installations in the same row with each other if it helps you organize them.
  11. Barney: Finally it’s my turn to get my goodies!
  12. Greg: Yes indeed, you get these four installations to help us all learn! Given the two placement rules we’ve learned, there are exactly four possible configurations for placing four installations on your installation mat (not counting which installation goes in which spot—just the overall possible shapes). Can you show all four possibilities?
  13. Barney: [shows all four legal configurations of their four installations; all other players help if desired; and congratulations all around once it’s been figured out]
  14. Greg: Great work! Choose one of those configurations and we’ll stick with it for the rest of the tutorial (this will all reset at the end of the tutorial so don’t worry too much about which one to choose).
  15. Greg: Now I want to go over the icons on these installations to help you get used to them. Note that there’s an icon reference panel on the polity screen of the player on your right.
  16. Greg: First, this is the start-of-gameicon. Installations and cards with this icon trigger at the start of each game. So Graham would start with two extra supply ships, and Barney would start with one extra drone. Vivien would choose whether to have the artificial intelligence slider start at +1 or –1 momentum.
  17. Greg: Similarly, this is the end-of-gameicon; installations and cards with this icon trigger at the start of each game. So Barney gets an extra Victory Pointat the end of every game; and if Vivien were to use this action spot, she’d get two extra VP as well at the end of this game.
  18. Greg: This exclamation pointindicates an effect that is triggered by a certain thing occurring. For example, whenever the number of months of a branch point decisionis 26 or higher, Graham gets to gain one drone from his reserve. Whenever a branch point decision causes the materials science slider to go up, Barney gains one supply ship from the central reserve.
  19. Greg: By the way, this little iconnext to the boxes is for something called “stage transitions”; you won’t have to worry about that for your first few games.
  20. Greg: Let’s continue with the instructions for envelope 02, and then we can talk more about action spots. The instructions say to put these four cards on some of these “story slots”; these are just example cards to help us learn the rules. Notice how the cards have these watermark iconsthat tell us which story slot each card goes on. Katie, could you read the Monstrous Solar Flare rule for us?
  21. Katie: [reads the text on the Monstrous Solar Flare card aloud]
  22. Greg: The icons make sense—this card is triggered by a certain thing occurring (and this installation icon tells us that it affects installations). Rule cards tend to have the full text of the rule, but the icons are there to help remind us that there’s some relevant rule to stay aware of.
  23. Greg: The other three rule cards all have action spots, which act like the action spots we’ve seen already on your installation mats and installations, as well as the Patent Office and Surveillance Network. Let’s go over some variations in how they work; I’ll have you read them aloud and then I’ll comment on them.
  24. Jason: [reads the text on the Cooperative Power card aloud]
  25. Greg: The single dronemeans you place one drone here when you take this action. Normally, that face-up drone would prevent anyone from taking that action during the same branch point vote; but here this globeicon overrides that—meaning that any number of players can take this action during the same branch point vote (but only one each per vote).
  26. Jason: And then at the end of the vote, they’d all get to reclaim their single drones from that card.
  27. Janelle: [reads the text on the Concentrated Manufacturing card aloud]
  28. Greg: Most actions have a predetermined number of drones you use; but this little triangle of dronesoverrides that—with a single action, you can put as many drones as you want on this card. (And then nobody could use that action again during that branch point vote.)
  29. Janelle: So because you always get one of your drones back at the end of the vote, this could be like trading one drone for two supply ships, or trading four drones for five supply ships, or whatever.
  30. Katie: [reads the text on the Sustainable Deployment card aloud]
  31. Greg: This card has “dark drone”icons on it—which we’ve actually seen already on these two installations. The number of dark drones indicates how many drones you need to use to take that action, just like with regular light drones; and similarly, all but one of the drones go into the player’s reserve at the end of the vote. But for dark drone actions, that last single drone isn’t recovered by the player; instead, it’s turned face down on the action spot and stays there for the rest of the game.
  32. Jason: So that means not only do dark drone actions “cost one more drone” than a corresponding light drone action, but also a dark drone action can only be used once per game, because that upside-drone keeps it blocked after it’s used?
  33. Greg: That’s right! And of course there are further variations—like Sustainable Deployment with its two dark drone action spots: that card can be used twice per game, in two different votes.
  34. Greg: I’ll bring one more action-spot icon to your attention—this stackicon that appears on your Drone Factories and Mining Operations. That icon means that drones are not automatically removed at the ends of votes—they can accumulate on the action spot over time.
  35. Greg: Any questions about any of these installations, cards, or icons?
  36. [after any questions have been brought up] Greg: Ok! The last line of this envelope’s instructions are to use these last two cards as the new branch point deck—still not shuffling because this is the tutorial.
