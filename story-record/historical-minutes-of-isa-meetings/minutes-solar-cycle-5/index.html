Minutes—Solar Cycle 5

This solar cycle was defined by restraint in the face of mounting uncertainty. Again and again, the ISA confronted opportunities for bold leaps—sending the Silgean through a temporal wormhole, overhauling communications with revolutionary theories, or rushing to exploit unfamiliar phenomena—but repeatedly chose to strengthen the foundations that would sustain exploration for decades to come. Materials science emerged as the quiet beneficiary of this philosophy, with sturdier spacecraft, industrial expansion, and resilient infrastructure gradually taking precedence over spectacular breakthroughs, while G'Dath Pumwi's new Station β seemed to embody the era's growing confidence in careful engineering. Yet caution did not bring calm. The worsening solar cycle compressed humanity's margin for error, bandwidth disputes spread from technical disagreements into diplomatic confrontations, and political movements increasingly turned scientific policy into matters of public identity. Even the ISA's response reflected this changing mood: one early attempt to organize a cooperative defense against the strengthening flares quickly gave way to polities fortifying their own prized facilities instead. By the cycle's end, humanity possessed stronger ships, broader observation networks, and a richer understanding of the frontier—but it also faced the unmistakable realization that scientific progress and political cohesion would no longer advance hand in hand.

Tekkalan's announcement that the distant wormhole branched through time electrified the ISA, and Malina Meriwa wasted little time championing the Silgean crew's request to enter it. Inuit's confidence in cybernetic exploration was unmistakable, but Stacey mounted an equally determined argument for sustained observation, warning that a phenomenon so poorly understood deserved years of careful study. Kalo Meme Momo joined Gupta in pressing for a permanent research presence, with Unitus von Standard-Issue lending more measured support. K‘Tinga spent much of the debate negotiating a future political obligation, while Silili Dashrapi concentrated on expanding Scythia's industrial and logistical capabilities. Malina's enthusiasm proved insufficient to overcome the coalition favoring caution. The Silgean settled into a stable orbit around the wormhole, and Gupta scientists soon took a prominent role in establishing the remarkable observatory that grew around it. Its success was celebrated throughout Gupta, where public enthusiasm for the project had become a rare source of unity across scientific disciplines and political interests.

Troubling reports from the Silgean's Sigfrodr drive brought the ISA's attention back to the practical difficulties of maintaining an expedition so far from home. Silili favored redistributing the drive's energy output to relieve its mounting stresses, but Malina argued forcefully that the ship needed a more substantial overhaul. Her position ultimately prevailed, and the Mayan fabrication specialist Tayute began preparing improved structural laminates for the drive housings, with materials researchers at Oyo's Station β contributing to their refinement. K‘Tinga meanwhile devoted attention to strengthening Oyo's research capacity, while Unitus took advantage of the distraction to redirect valuable Gupta shipments toward Achaemenia. The growing danger of solar flares also intruded upon the proceedings. Kalo made an initial contribution toward a coordinated defense of humanity's installations, but Tu’i Tonga soon joined Gupta and Scythia in fortifying its own facilities instead. The shared effort languished, leaving Tayute's successful redesign as the meeting's principal achievement. His improvements would eventually find their way into spacecraft throughout the interpolity fleets.

Fresh projections of worsening solar activity brought an unusually sharp disagreement over how humanity should prepare for the coming decades. K‘Tinga argued vigorously for hardened materials capable of protecting vulnerable electronics, an approach Kalo also supported. Unitus countered with an ambitious proposal to station cybernetically enhanced observers throughout the inner solar system, insisting that earlier warnings would give threatened installations precious additional time to prepare. Stacey and Silili offered more modest support for Achaemenia's position, while Malina concentrated on protecting Inuit's own facilities and expanding its productive resources. Gupta also continued its program of voluntary genetic adjustments. The ISA ultimately approved the observer network, drawing on research from Igaluk Qillaq's Station α and the surgical expertise of Qaumaniq, whose facility operated there under Achaemenian authority. The arrangement gave Unitus a direct institutional interest in the project's success, although Qaumaniq's rapidly growing surgical practice remained closely connected to Inuit cybernetics. Enhanced observers soon began taking their posts across the inner solar system, including at Neil's meticulously maintained lunar installation. Their reports confirmed an alarming increase in sunspot activity, raising fears that the devastating blackouts marking the ends of solar cycles might arrive progressively earlier.

The growing fleet of AI explorers beyond the Kauppias Belt presented the ISA with a difficult question about the limits of its responsibilities. K‘Tinga advocated greater independence for distant vessels through local material-to-energy conversion, arguing that explorers should not remain indefinitely dependent on resources transmitted from home. Silili Dashrapi delivered one of her strongest interventions of the cycle in opposition, insisting that humanity's most adventurous scientific expeditions deserved the communications capacity necessary to continue their work. Stacey supported Scythia's position, while Malina secured a future political favor and Kalo assumed the office of Patent Agent. Unitus largely avoided the scientific dispute, concentrating instead on expanding Achaemenia's logistical resources and appropriating shipments intended for Oyo. K‘Tinga continued building Oyo's industrial capacity, but the ISA ultimately endorsed Silili's proposal. Energy allocations to distant relay stations increased, and AI vessels began exploring regions previously beyond reliable communication. The resulting congestion soon reached ordinary users, however, provoking resentment over privileged access to bandwidth. Tensions worsened when Tu’i Tonga expelled Han scientists from a conference in Fonua, prompting retaliatory tariffs on Tu’i Tongan ship hulls. Even Neil's lunar station became increasingly valuable as an independent communications and tracking outpost, though its precise schedules offered little protection against the wider network's mounting delays.

Fola Fuar's unexpected passage around Jupiter and into the inner solar system prompted an exceptionally intense debate. Unitus von Standard-Issue committed Achaemenia's considerable diplomatic influence to waiting for the latest artificial intelligence to acquire the necessary remote-reprogramming capabilities. Silili and Stacey agreed, though neither approached Unitus's level of determination. Malina Meriwa and K‘Tinga mounted an equally vigorous campaign for immediate investigation, arguing that the comet's unusual trajectory offered an opportunity that might disappear before the newer technology was ready. K‘Tinga also approached Kalo privately, securing Tu’i Tonga's support through a substantial exchange of favors. The resulting coalition prevailed despite Unitus's strenuous objections. Scythia meanwhile completed another stage in the genetic development of its population. The existing metal-extraction AI, subsequently known as Skaram, soon established itself on Fola Fuar and began examining the comet's extraordinary density and unidentified metallic composition. G'Dath Pumwi's materials researchers at Station β took particular interest in the incoming measurements, which suggested that the strange object might contain substances unlike anything previously encountered in the solar system.

The discovery of remarkably preserved insect fossils at Malmani-era excavation sites brought an unexpected personal dimension to the ISA's deliberations. K‘Tinga argued with extraordinary persistence that researchers should concentrate on the organic traces, which might reveal the biological composition of organisms associated with the ancient prophets. G'Dath Pumwi, whose enthusiasm for insects had survived his reluctant rise through Oyo's scientific administration, followed the discoveries with particular fascination. Yet Malina, Stacey, and Kalo favored a different approach: using cybernetically enhanced researchers to reconstruct the fossils' intricate internal structures. Silili offered quieter support, while Unitus reclaimed the office of Patent Agent and expanded Achaemenia's logistical resources. Inuit continued refining its population's genetic characteristics, and Gupta strengthened its research capacity. The cybernetic proposal prevailed, bringing Igaluk Qillaq's Station α into close collaboration with excavation teams. Their investigations uncovered microscopic mechanical structures embedded within the ancient insects, revealing an astonishingly sophisticated system for distributing engineered genetic material through natural ecosystems. Parallel studies in other regions uncovered evidence of coordinated airborne and aquatic delivery mechanisms. The findings suggested that the Malmani prophets had once employed an integrated cybernetic ecology of extraordinary precision, leaving modern researchers struggling to understand how its components had been controlled.

By the cycle's final major debate, the communications crisis had become a source of open diplomatic rivalry. Kalo Meme Momo vigorously championed theoretical research into an entirely new form of wireless communication, arguing that humanity needed a fundamental breakthrough rather than another round of industrial expansion. Malina and K‘Tinga instead favored increasing semiconductor production, with Oyo's Station β particularly well positioned to contribute improvements in materials and cooling systems. The ensuing negotiations centered on Unitus, who received competing approaches from Inuit, Oyo, and Tu’i Tonga with a succession of impassive acknowledgments. Kalo eventually secured Achaemenia's support through an especially generous arrangement, prompting Unitus to speak in favor of the theoretical initiative. Malina responded by canvassing the ISA for additional support and eventually persuaded Unitus to refrain from further intervention. Throughout the maneuvering, Silili concentrated on strengthening Scythia's logistical position, while Stacey expanded Gupta's productive capacity before claiming the office of Patent Agent. Achaemenia also completed another stage in its voluntary genetic program. K‘Tinga and Malina maintained their pressure until the ISA endorsed expanded semiconductor manufacturing, providing Station β with a substantial new industrial undertaking. Incan architects soon proposed larger transmission towers, but the anticipated improvements did little to quiet public anger. Political agitators stirred protests between neighboring polities, and reports of unauthorized bandwidth diversions along the Scythia–Theodosia border suggested that the struggle over communications infrastructure was entering a more confrontational phase.
