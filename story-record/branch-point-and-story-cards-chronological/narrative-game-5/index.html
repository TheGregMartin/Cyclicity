Narrative—Game 5

Achaemenia’s Lunar Station edict

The AI celebrity Tekkalan reports that incredibly, cosmologists now realize that the Silgean's energy readings can be explained by the wormhole branching, not through space, but through time! Do we grant the cybernetic crew's request to send the Silgean daringly through the temporal branch, or have the ship station itself there indefinitely to investigate its radiation profile?
—While their boldness is admirable, the dangers of a hasty transit through a new and poorly understood pathway are far too great. We instruct the Silgean to move itself into position for constant monitoring and study of this unparalleled cosmic object, expanding its observation schedule and reallocating onboard systems to maintain uninterrupted measurements over extended intervals.

Science at the Edge. The Silgean settles into a fixed orbit around the wormhole, its motion guided by a lattice of synchronized drones. Construction unfolds with the curiosity characteristic of Gupta's embrace of the cerebral. Specialists from every field converge, trading equations and ideas as easily as conversation, turning discovery itself into a shared language. The growing outpost gleams with elegant geometry, its modules arranged like verses in an unfinished poem\[LongDash]each measuring a different aspect of the wormhole's flux, together forming a coherent rhythm of observation. The data it gathers quickly redefines humanity's understanding of spacetime, not through force or fortune, but through the art of structured insight. At the frontier of the unknown, the pursuit of knowledge becomes its own graceful architecture.

After prolonged continuous use, the Silgean's Sigfrodr drive shows irregular power cycling, with radiation exhaust rising beyond projected limits and slight biochemical decay detected in the onboard algae scrubbers. Do we redistribute drive output through an adjusted energy flow that balances thermal load, or recall the ship to a Mayan outpost for a full redesign of its drive housings using improved structural laminates?
—Long-term safety depends on building strength into the frame. A fabrication specialist named Tayute refits the Silgean's drive housings with graded laminates that flex under stress and channel radiation into insulated layers. His overhaul delays the next mission, but results in a sturdier design adopted across interpolity flotillas.

The already powerful solar flares are predicted to strengthen even further in the coming years, with modeling suggesting broader radiation fronts and less predictable onset. Infrastructure planners warn that both ground-based systems and orbital platforms will face mounting strain as cycles intensify. Do we develop hardened materials to shield valuable electronic installations, or deploy cybernetically enhanced watchers for early warnings of flare-ups?
—The sooner we know a solar flare is coming, the better prepared we'll be for its effects. We fund a rebate program for ISA contractors willing to obtain wide-spectrum vision implants and situate them both in Earth's orbit and throughout the inner solar system. These human sentinels extend warning coverage beyond instruments alone, even as the margin for response continues to narrow.

Who Watches the Watchers? With reasonable efficiency, waves of spacefarers return to Earth to avail themselves of the ocular cybernetics subsidized by the ISA. Most of them obtain their enhancements from a skilled surgeon named Qaumaniq, the leader of a rapidly growing Inuit facility. Her operations become known for speedy recovery times that allow the beneficiaries to quickly return to their observation posts in various orbits, providing additional warning of solar dangers. But the ISA hears bad news as well, confirmed across multiple independent observatories: the increase in solar flares is being produced by an elevated rate of sunspot generation. Experts fear that along with more dangerous solar flares in the middle of solar cycles, the devastating global blackouts at the cycles' ends will start to happen sooner and sooner in the years to come, compressing humanity's margin for preparation.

A growing cadre of AI vessels ventures ever farther into the outer solar system, their ships fanning out through regions where signal lag and dwindling power make each decision slower and more perilous. We must choose whether to bolster them from afar or demand greater self-sufficiency. Do we require them to convert local materials into energy sources, or use ISA energy reserves to amplify their bandwidth?
—We are here to support promising scientific ventures, not strand them at the edge of reach. We allocate a larger portion of our central energy reserves to long-range transmission and tracking stations, extending relay capacity beyond the Kauppias Belt and enabling those AI-controlled ships to explore at greater range\[LongDash]even as the added load tightens margins elsewhere in the network.

Science Becomes Political. Replete with energy and bandwidth, artificial-intelligence ships push themselves to the limits, scouting the farthest corners of the solar system for resources and scientific anomalies. The most innovative thinkers among this informal fleet even coordinate their early plans to push beyond the limits of the system into deep space. However, ISA's preferential treatment of AI has downsides as well. Bandwidth allocation queues grow visible to ordinary users, with throttled connections and dropped packets growing more common. Funding for experimental power slows to a trickle, angering certain polities whose research grinds to a halt. Tu'i Tonga causes an international incident by deporting Han scientists from a conference in Fonua, and Han responds with punitive tariffs on Tu'i Tongan ship hulls. All around the world, tempers are wearing thin as these "bandwidth wars" multiply.

The engineer Itami reports that Fola Fuar took an unexpected turn around Jupiter and is now orbiting among the inner planets. We can remotely reprogram the comet's probe, but the latest artificial intelligence hasn't had remote reprogramming implemented yet. Do we wait for the newest AI to be upgraded, or press an existing metal-extraction AI version into service?
—We need data on this strangely agile comet as soon as possible. Using an AI version that can eventually be reprogrammed remotely will provide months, if not years, of data we would otherwise miss as Fola Fuar returns to Earth's neighbourhood, giving our analysts a broader baseline long before the newer software is ready.

Skaram's Distant Office. Before long the artificial intelligence controlling the probe, which was customized by Han astronomers and chose the name Skaram, has secured aits vehicle to the Fola Fuar surface and is diligently analyzing all the data ait can collect. While aits programming is not perfect, Skaram is able to modify aitself to some extent. Ait divides aits time among analyzing the comet's physical structure\[LongDash]extremely solid and dense, with a high metal content that defies initial identification\[LongDash]sorting through the faint energy fluxes surrounding the comet, and testing theories for the comet's origin and movements against simulations from fundamental physics. Occasional interference from the comet's own charged particles flickers across Skaram's sensors, adding texture to a dataset already more complex than any probe has gathered so far.

Field teams are reporting unfamiliar molecular residues and microstructures from excavation sites associated with Malmani-era civic centers. Do we concentrate on analyzing organic traces that might reveal their biological composition, or investigate fossilized insect remains using researchers' own cybernetic implants, to test new methods for structural imaging and sensory reconstruction?
—These fossilized insects could inform new analytical techniques. We support a pilot study in which field specialists integrate their neural-visual implants with precision imaging tools, allowing direct sensory mapping of the specimens' internal structures. The objective is to refine data-capture fidelity and explore whether hybrid sensory analysis can reveal details conventional microscopy might overlook.

High-traffic AI entities continue to choke neighboring networks, slowing data transfers and triggering disputes between industrial and civilian users. The ISA is faced with a choice between incremental growth or radical innovation. Do we support new semiconductor production to steadily increase the network's capacity, or invest in theorists beginning to propose a completely new form of wireless communication?
—Supply and demand have always caught with each other in the past. We boost our existing support for the components industry, expanding chip foundries and upgrading cooling systems to push production past current limits. The backlog eases, but only slightly, and the competing sectors grumble that no matter how much bandwidth grows, someone always finds a way to fill it.

Waiting for the Network. Staying the course with steady support for existing industry seems to have paid off: Incan engineer-architects have drawn up scaled-up schematics for larger high-traffic transmission towers. Given the effort and materials needed for each tower, it remains to be seen how fast they can be manufactured and installed. Meanwhile, the social influencers known as Bakht4Umayyad and Kiran4Gupta are starting to spur substantial protests against neighboring polities, making the Achaemenian and Seljuk central leadership feel that they are forced to push back as a result. Even more worryingly, the ISA is starting to hear of repeated incidents on the border between Scythia and Theodosia, in which people from one polity make unauthorized alterations to infrastructure to divert bandwidth from their neighbor.
