Narrative—Game 2

You Scratch My Back…. The Interpolity Science Assembly's inaugural decade felt optimistic, but anxieties resurfaced with the destructive close of the first sunspot cycle. The nine subordinate polities sought reassurance and advantage by courting allies among the six ISA members, often sweetening their approaches with hints of aid or outright bribes. Favors became occasional exchange—technical data shared discreetly, shipments redirected in goodwill, partnerships quietly negotiated beyond formal oversight. While the ISA polities generally declined such offers in the name of fairness, moments of overextension sometimes made the lure of extra resources too tempting to resist, even when entangled with conditions….

The Oyo materials specialist Omotanwa argues that the abrupt human rejuvenation seen in the record may have arisen from environmental catalysts rather than purely genetic change. Do we ask her to expand genetic reconstruction to search for synthetic sequences introduced during the Malmani era, or investigate environmental factors through energy mapping of preserved habitats?
—Biology is the place to look for traces of change. Omotanwa partners with a Scythian molecular historian named Spalahores to extract partial genomes from Malmani-era marrow samples. The data reveals an unclassified cluster active only in that century. Its structure links immunity, growth, and cellular repair in unexpected ways, suggesting a global shift in how longevity itself was regulated.

Given that Malmani did truly walk among the early polities, traces of their speech or writing must remain. Do we direct the Aksum historian Hiwot to expand the ISA's cross-polity linguistics program to map shared inscriptions and sacred texts, or develop AI-assisted imaging and pigment analyses to study the physical composition and age of those writings?
—Words and syntax carry the fingerprints of contact. We assemble linguists, mathematicians, and cultural historians to align fragments of Achaemenian, Scythian, and Mayan text within a shared semantic framework. Overlapping idioms emerge, recurring imagery repeating across oceans, giving the sense that the past may have spoken with one voice.

Echoes Across Empires. A Scythian linguist named Hajatria compiles the first unified corpus of Malmani-era writings, merging inscriptions and ritual texts from Achaemenia, Scythia, and Maya. His statistical parsing reveals near-identical phrasing in hymns, oaths, and astronomical tables—verses recurring across two impossibly isolated continents. Indeed, the alignment is unnervingly precise: stylistic markers, ink composition, and script evolution all point to creation within the same decade, a synchrony that strains accepted chronologies. Official reports present the data without comment, folded into the growing consensus that Malmani's teachings spread almost instantly through early civilization. The discovery electrifies the scholarly community and strengthens public conviction that humanity's first age of enlightenment was real, deliberate, and shared.

The Achaemenian pilot Amytis is eager to personally investigate the Olwyn Cloud's uneven structure, arguing that direct traversal will reveal what distant measurements miss. Others counter that careful gravitational analysis could explain the pattern without committing scarce exploratory resources. Do we stimulate improved energy sources for Amytis's engine, or prioritize theoretical modeling?
—An unexpected discovery requires new theoretical insight. After thanking Amytis for her service, we shift funding away from the low-albedo implant program toward physicists investigating possible causes of the underand over-populated regions within the Olwyn Cloud, consolidating models across multiple datasets.

Listening to Tekkalan. Research groups around the world immediately start to debate the possible causes of this non-uniformity within the Olwyn Cloud. One Seljuk team, working in close collaboration with an AI connected to their full theoretical knowledge database, emerges from the pack with a promising explanation: the presence of Ellertin–Roinn bridges, popularly known as wormholes, subtly reshaping local distributions. Iterative simulations show how even a small number of such structures could produce the observed clustering without disturbing the wider system. Before long the AI, which chooses the name Tekkalan, becomes something of a public figure; aits tireless willingness to explain the evolving status of the gravitational calculations, fielding questions from specialists and curious observers alike, draws widespread attention to the once-remote edge of the solar system.

During a solo mapping sortie within the Olwyn Cloud, the Achaemenian pilot Amytis feels faint rhythmic pulses through her suit's feedback sensors—oscillations matching the spacing of nearby proto-cometary clusters. Do we recall her to upgrade her flight rig with cybernetic amplifiers to translate these pulses into navigational data, or dispatch a harmonic cartographer from Gupta to analyze the field patterns and model their origin remotely?
—If sensation carries information, the pilot should feel it fully. We retrofit Amytis's suit with neural-haptic relays tuned to ambient field pressure. The amplifiers render each pulse as tactile signal, letting her trace gradients by touch alone. The new interface maps composition shifts ten times faster than optical methods.

As the Seljuk team's wormhole theory dominates attention, the Tu'i Tongan planetary scientist Fahuiki urges direct observation to test the idea, though the necessary drives and detectors remain unproven. Do we authorize a ready field experiment using existing energy infrastructure to search for resonance signatures, or allocate time for a full theoretical rederivation of the bridge model incorporating new genetic and chemical data from captured protocomet samples?
—Real certainty takes time to build. We allow Fahuiki's team to reconstruct the model from first principles, integrating isotopic traces from protocomet organics with the Seljuk data. The work proceeds slowly, but the final formulation links bridge formation to matter density, giving the theory a physical footing that energy alone could never prove.

With the rapid expansion of affordable biomechanical implants, AI-controlled maintenance and mining vehicles are starting to report increased transmission lags as more signals compete for the same channels. Operators warn that coordination delays are beginning to ripple through logistics networks. Do we prioritize available bandwidth for cybernetically enhanced individuals, or reserve capacity for construction and commercial usage?
—The ready availability of cybernetics is entirely due to the economy driven by these vehicles. We establish regulations that preserve a protected band of frequencies for construction and commercial fleets, keeping supply chains predictable and coordinated even under heavy load. We reassure our populaces that supply will soon catch up to the suddenly increased demand.

Anti-Cyber Propaganda. With the increased bandwidth allocated by the ISA, commercial and industrial enterprises thrive. Assembly plants step up their resource delivery networks to continually innovate with new alloys and solvents, aided by an expanding AI-driven exploration of the solar system that feeds designs back into production almost as quickly as they are tested. Back on Earth, however, the limited communication capacity impacts common folks; implant syncing becomes slower and less dependable, and tensions rise within affected populations. An influencer using the name Bakht4Umayyad gathers a burgeoning following by stoking rumours of Seljuk universities promoting anti-cyber philosophies and deliberately suppressing opposing points of views. The erosion of trust arising in border communities around the globe presents a worrying trend, as everyday frustrations harden into lasting suspicion.

As Hajatria's linguistic analysis expands, his instruments begin exceeding their energy allotments: decades-old power grids strain to support simultaneous acoustic imaging and ink-spectral correlation. Do we commission new energy relays and stabilizers to sustain the project, or install an intelligent distribution system to balance power dynamically across the archive network?
—An Incan powerwright named Zarama argues that lasting insight demands lasting current. We direct him to construct microturbine relays dedicated to the linguistic labs. The reinforcement restores capacity and stabilizes long-session imaging. Hajatria's teams finish their comparative scans, though total consumption power now rivals an orbital station's.

When the Achaemenian pilot Amytis begins relaying her navigation data from Sigfrodr-drive flights through the Olwyn Cloud, the AI Tekkalan publicizes faint irregularities in her recorded trajectories—minor shifts that appear consistent with hidden gravitational nodes. Do we assign Tekkalan to autonomously model these deviations from current data, or offer Amytis and other pilots reinforced cybernetic limbs capable of registering the nodes' microforces directly through physical feedback?
—Pilots feel what sensors only measure. An Inuit cyberneticist named Uukkarnit designs actuator braces that transmit drive-field stress as measurable force to the pilots' limbs. Amytis reports the impulses registering like shallow tides against her frame, guiding her through the invisible curvature of space before her instruments even respond.
