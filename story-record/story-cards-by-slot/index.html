2nd slot  

Summary: The ISA’s restoration of the world’s archives under Omotanwa preserves humanity’s accumulated knowledge and recovers vast quantities of forgotten data, creating the most complete historical record in generations. That record quickly produces transformative results: researchers confirm that human lifespans increased dramatically during the Malmani era, establishing it as a real historical period rather than legend, while Hajatria’s analysis of writings from Achaemenia, Scythia, and Maya reveals that Malmani teachings appeared across distant civilizations with impossible speed and synchronization. Together, these discoveries convince both scholars and the public that humanity’s first age of enlightenment was a genuine, global phenomenon whose influence reshaped the world within a remarkably short span of time.

1: Archives Reborn. Across the world’s archives, the great retrofit begins. Libraries, databanks, and memory vaults are stripped of vulnerable circuitry and reborn with magnetoresistant cores designed to preserve humanity’s knowledge for centuries. Omotanwa, an Oyo materials architect celebrated for transforming industrial compounds into instruments of endurance, oversees the global conversion from orbit. Her teams move with a precision that mirrors her own calm authority; each upgrade not only repairs a system but honors the generations that built it. As ancient servers flicker back to life beneath their new shielding, long-lost datasets and early records rejoin the living network. What began as a desperate safeguard becomes an act of renewal, binding the scattered history of humanity into a single, resilient memory.

2: True Beginnings. Year-long ISA projects across multiple polities converge on a single result. Genetic sampling from burial sites, medical relics, and preserved tissues shows that human lifespans lengthened sharply between twenty and ten centuries ago, rising across continents within just a few generations. Parallel isotope studies trace abrupt global surges in vitality, confirming this pattern. The data is too consistent for coincidence, too rapid for evolution, and too uniform for regional innovation. Within the ISA, quiet unease tempers the triumph—no mechanism explains how this change occurred. Still, the public is euphoric as newsfeeds proclaim the “Malmani era” real history, not parable. Spiritual communities hold vigils to honor proof that enlightenment once walked the Earth, and the ISA basks in global acclaim for settling the question that defined an age.

3: Echoes Across Empires. A Scythian linguist named Hajatria compiles the first unified corpus of Malmani-era writings, merging inscriptions and ritual texts from Achaemenia, Scythia, and Maya. His statistical parsing reveals near-identical phrasing in hymns, oaths, and astronomical tables—verses recurring across two impossibly isolated continents. Indeed, the alignment is unnervingly precise: stylistic markers, ink composition, and script evolution all point to creation within the same decade, a synchrony that strains accepted chronologies. Official reports present the data without comment, folded into the growing consensus that Malmani's teachings spread almost instantly through early civilization. The discovery electrifies the scholarly community and strengthens public conviction that humanity's first age of enlightenment was real, deliberate, and shared.

3rd slot  

Summary: The discovery of the Olwyn Cloud reveals that the outer edge of the solar system is shaped by hidden structure rather than chance. Amytis’s observations lead Tekkalan to identify wormholes as the most likely explanation for the Cloud’s unusual composition, while Liu Youwei’s self-healing LY-hulls make long-range exploration practical. When the Silgean enters one of these wormholes, its reports confirm that most star systems are linked by similar networks, opening a vast web of destinations beyond the solar system. Humanity’s attention turns from merely reaching the outer system to exploring an interconnected galaxy whose pathways had remained invisible until now.

1: Patches in the Cloud. Before long, firsthand surveillance of the distant edge of the system confirms that our solar system is surrounded by a vast torus of proto-cometary bodies, a hundred times more distant than Pluto and the Kauppias Belt of asteroids. This hypothetical region had been named the Olwyn Cloud by the Global Astronomical Society, but now it has been directly observed. As reports arrive from scattered patrols and long-range surveys, the scale of the structure becomes undeniable. After several months of aggregating other pilots’ data, one of the first to receive the optical implant, an Achaemenian named Amytis, notices that while the proto-comets display a wide variety of mineral compositions, the more unusual materials are consistently concentrated in certain patches of the Olwyn Cloud; this lack of uniformity cries out for an explanation.

2: Listening to Tekkalan. Research groups around the world immediately start to debate the possible causes of this non-uniformity within the Olwyn Cloud. One Seljuk team, working in close collaboration with an AI connected to their full theoretical knowledge database, emerges from the pack with a promising explanation: the presence of Ellertin–Roinn bridges, popularly known as wormholes, subtly reshaping local distributions. Iterative simulations show how even a small number of such structures could produce the observed clustering without disturbing the wider system. Before long the AI, which chooses the name Tekkalan, becomes something of a public figure; aits tireless willingness to explain the evolving status of the gravitational calculations, fielding questions from specialists and curious observers alike, draws widespread attention to the once-remote edge of the solar system.

3: Self-Healing Hulls. After some time, we receive positive news from Cangmang Station, an advanced Han shipyard in Earth orbit. Its director Liu Youwei led the development of a new hull-plate substance made from germanium nanotubes threaded with ionomeric polymers; her material is able to repair itself in real time, making it ideal for resisting micropunctures. Test sections seal visible damage within seconds under simulated debris strikes, leaving only faint thermal traces behind. As a result, spaceships equipped with these “LY-hulls” are much lighter and thus capable of carrying more fuel for longer journeys into the outer system. Meanwhile, Tekkalan has been controlling aits impatience, as ait has pieced together strong circumstantial evidence of a large wormhole in the Olwyn Cloud in the direction of the galactic centre; the ISA is eager for firsthand confirmation.

4: Wormhole Explorers. Some two years or so after ISA approval, a massive Sigfrodr-drive vessel, the “Silgean”, passes through the most promising wormhole with its combination of human and AI crew. Fortunately messages can be sent faithfully back through the wormholes, and while the reports and discoveries are unpredictable, the information never fails to fascinate experts and laypeople alike. In addition to resource-rich bodies in orbit around stars, and planets and moons with varying suitability for human occupation, we learn that most star systems possess a cluster of wormholes on their own outer edges, through which even more stars can be reached. Despite occasional dead-ends and backtracking, the continuing exploration of the huge ship greatly increases humanity's motivation for expanding beyond our solar system.

4th slot 

1: Anti-Cyber Propaganda. With the increased bandwidth allocated by the ISA, commercial and industrial enterprises thrive. Assembly plants step up their resource delivery networks to continually innovate with new alloys and solvents, aided by an expanding AI-driven exploration of the solar system that feeds designs back into production almost as quickly as they are tested. Back on Earth, however, the limited communication capacity impacts common folks; implant syncing becomes slower and less dependable, and tensions rise within affected populations. An influencer using the name Bakht4Umayyad gathers a burgeoning following by stoking rumours of Seljuk universities promoting anti-cyber philosophies and deliberately suppressing opposing points of views. The erosion of trust arising in border communities around the globe presents a worrying trend, as everyday frustrations harden into lasting suspicion.

5th slot 

1: A Telescopic Expert. One of the experts in orbital mechanics, a Mayan named Xaman, successfully applies for a position on the ORI. They bring up from Earth an intrasystem telescope, linked to the world’s best astronomical databases, that can automatically track the movements of thousands of masses simultaneously. The instrument’s first calibration sweep lights up the ORI’s displays with shifting vectors from satellites, debris, and distant survey craft. Xaman will check in on the comet, which the Global Astronomical Society has named Fola Fuar, from time to time to confirm the experts’ optimism about its path; but most of their work will be concerned with other orbiting bodies.

Bottom slot 

0: A New Dawn Above Earth. At the beginning of a sunspot cycle, the Interpolity Science Association convenes in orbit for the first time. Its headquarters is a well-appointed suite of rooms in the Orbital Research Installation, a glimmering ring suspended high above the quiet curve of Earth. Envoys from six polities have been chosen to lead civilization through an age of discovery—and of growing peril. The Sun, once a symbol of renewal, has begun to falter in strange and unpredictable rhythms, casting flares that disrupt communication, damage infrastructure, and darken continents. Yet optimism runs high; humanity has never been so united or so capable. As this 1st Cycle begins, hope rises with it that understanding the rhythms of the star will illuminate the path ahead. Here aboard the ORI, science and diplomacy intertwine, and every decision the ISA makes shapes the survival of civilization.
